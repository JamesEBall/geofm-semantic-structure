title: Additional geographic support before global model scoring
author: James Edward Ball
date: 2026-10-15
---

The initial 120-block acquisition produced 22,720 accepted scenes from 24,000 attempts, with 1,280 quality exclusions and no technical failures. Admission failed for built-up validation scenes (20, required 30), wetland test blocks (5, required 15), built-up test blocks (9, required 15) and bare-ground test blocks (13, required 15). The original inventory, failed report and source freezes are preserved in the dated amendment archive. No global image features or model scores have been produced.

Add new blocks using the same WorldCover reference and the same acquisition code. Preserve every original block, split assignment, retained scene and exclusion. The fixed additional support targets are four validation blocks containing built-up candidates, and 18 wetland, 12 built-up and four bare-ground test blocks. A test block can supply more than one target, so the maximum is 38 additional blocks. These targets include a reserve for the existing image-quality exclusions; they do not change the admission thresholds.

Enumerate the original one-degree land-centre grid between 55 degrees south and 75 degrees north from the stored Natural Earth geographic groups. Exclude centres within 200 km of any original block or within 100 km of any prior study point. Within each geographic group, sort by SHA-256 of the fixed augmentation seed and integer coordinates, then interleave the six groups in their original order. Screen this sequence in fixed batches and retain the same ordering regardless of worker completion time. A new block must also remain at least 200 km from each previously added block.

Use a 128 by 128 nearest-neighbour WorldCover overview of the original block interior for a coarse screen. At least eight pixels of a target class are required before running the unchanged candidate selector. This screen reduces reference-map traffic and does not admit images. The original selector must return exactly 200 candidates, with at least ten wetland or built-up candidates, or 20 bare-ground candidates, for the corresponding support target. The original 900 m neighbourhood, 80% candidate purity and 1 km candidate separation remain unchanged. Keep every screened result and its candidate counts.

Assign the first four qualifying built-up blocks to validation. Subsequent qualifying blocks enter the test split if they help meet an outstanding target. Count all qualifying test categories in each selected block, and stop at the fixed additional targets. Freeze the resulting inventory before fetching any new Sentinel-2 image windows. All 200 original-rule candidates in every added block are attempted; selection is independent of image embeddings and language-matching outcomes. Exhaustion or any unresolved technical failure stops preparation.

The original per-window gates remain: dominant reference fraction at least 60%, excluded or missing reference fraction at most 10%, at least 98% valid SCL vegetation, bare-ground or water pixels, six valid bands including B8A, checked scale and offset, and finite reflectance in [0,2]. The original date range, acquisition ranking, catalogue checks and 24-acquisition limit remain unchanged. The final admission still requires at least 30 scenes across three blocks per category for training and validation, and at least 200 scenes across 15 blocks per category for testing. It also checks source-item split isolation, 5 km cross-split scene separation, duplicates, all checksums and zero unresolved technical failures.

Only successful admission can release the global GPU array. The original cohort failure remains part of the research record. The augmented cohort is a reference-stratified geographic stress test; the added blocks change regional and class sampling proportions and do not support claims about representative global prevalence. Report the original and additional sampling counts separately. No encoder, language reference, readout setting, null comparison or analysis endpoint changes in this amendment.
