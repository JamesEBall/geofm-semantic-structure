# Support-aware geometry calibration amendment

Author: James Edward Ball. Declared 18 October 2026 before this amendment's simulation outcomes. Status: design for independent review; no empirical interval admitted.

## Reason and preserved evidence

The preceding frozen geographic-cell percentile bootstrap completed 7,000 records and failed population coverage. Its protocol, code, records and original study gates remain unchanged. A post-hoc reconstruction of basic intervals on those old draws is development evidence only, recorded by the parent task. It motivates a predeclared new candidate; it supplies no confirmation data. The empirical analysis is also being corrected to apply the retained-window mask and the full-taxonomy family exclusions. This amendment uses retained metadata only and never reads empirical image-language correlations to select a method or simulation parameter.

The primary candidate is the basic interval with positive geographic-cell weights. The same draws give a percentile comparator. A second, separately reported basic interval shares positive weights over fixed five-degree spatial blocks. Its percentile counterpart is also reported. No method is selected by its simulated or empirical effect size. Each method retains its declared inferential scope and failures. No correction factor, class deletion, pseudocount, successful-draw filter or fitted shrinkage is added after viewing outcomes.

## Population and selection boundary

The immutable `RETAINED-DESIGN.json` records all retained one-degree cells, their train/validation/test split, R1 admitted-class window counts, ten-degree geographic histogram bins and five-degree spatial blocks. It contains 188 classes and their actual frozen family IDs. There are 6,689 training, 922 validation and 1,783 test cells, including retained cells without an admitted R1 positive. The positive test subset contains 1,718 cells and 8,755 window-class pairs. Per-class test-cell support has minimum five, median 22 and maximum 311. The five source-table hashes are saved with the design. No representation features, language similarities or model outcomes enter this export.

Fresh-sample scenarios draw each split's observed number of cells independently and uniformly, with replacement, from that split's retained cell templates. Each draw denotes a new geographic sampling unit with the template's class co-occurrence, per-class window counts and region. Duplicate template indices therefore get separate cell identities and separate cell noise; they share region effects only in the declared spatial scenarios. This is an explicit finite-template population conditional on the original acquisition, cap and retained sampling design. It does not model uncapped OSM prevalence, arbitrary new regions or all possible concepts. The original 150-window target-class cap is already present in the template frequencies; it is not reapplied to the synthetic fresh draws, which would change the target. The protocol therefore does not claim coverage over repeating the entire uncapped acquisition-and-cap pipeline.

The original category inventory was admitted partly from test support. For every fresh repetition, compute the original admission thresholds on the sampled counts: at least 30 training, five validation, ten test windows, five test cells and eight total cells per class. Keep all 188 classes in every target. Record each class's admission result and whether all 188 pass. Report unconditional coverage over all repetitions, and coverage conditional on all-class admission as a separate diagnostic with its actual denominator. Never redraw until admission succeeds. Missing classes or undefined geometry count as unavailable intervals and misses in unconditional coverage. New admission failures do not silently reduce the vocabulary. Conditional-on-admission coverage cannot replace unconditional coverage.

A fixed-design diagnostic holds all retained template identities and counts fixed and draws only new feature noise. Its target averages that same fixed geographic design. The proposed weighting adds variation over the design beyond this noise-only repeated-sampling model, so this diagnostic may be conservative or miscalibrated even when fresh-sample inference differs. It measures a narrower uncertainty source and cannot qualify an interval for new locations. An acquisition diagnostic draws four times as many independent cells in every split from the same template population. It preserves the class inventory and latent geometry. This is a hypothetical support budget, not an authorised acquisition or a claim that the original cap permits those additional observations.

## Known data-generating population

Dimension is 24. Generate fixed appearance A and semantic S class vectors as sqrt(0.6) times an independent standard-normal family vector plus sqrt(0.4) times an independent class vector. V is an independent class vector shared by the paired routes. Each occupied ten-degree bin has a fixed standard-normal location vector L. These objects use a fixed world seed, shared across all regimes and beta values; no world is screened by its geometry.

For class c in evaluation cell g with template window count n_gc, conditional image means are A_c + 0.7 V_c + 0.6 L_g for the baseline and that same mean plus beta S_c for the semantic route. Appearance mean is A_c + 0.3 L_g. Add image noise comprising a shared cell vector with coefficient 0.7, a shared cell-family vector with coefficient 0.5, an independent route/class-cell vector with coefficient 0.5, and an independent route/window vector averaged over the n_gc physical windows (one shared vector for every class labelling the same window). The first two terms are shared between routes. Appearance noise has independent cell, cell-family and window-mean coefficients 0.4, 0.3 and 0.7/sqrt(n_gc). All innovation vectors are standard normal and mean zero. Window innovations are independent between physical windows and shared across co-occurring labels on the same window. Their class-cell averages preserve the resulting covariance and are exact sufficient statistics for these Gaussian windows.

The standard regime multiplies all noise terms by one. The low-noise regime uses 0.5. The heterogeneous regime additionally multiplies each class's noise by sqrt(1 + 22/s_c), where s_c is its retained test-cell support, fixed before outcomes. This stresses rare-class estimation without reading observed effects. In the spatial-five regime, add an image region vector with coefficient 0.7 shared by all cells and both routes in the same fixed five-degree block, and an independent appearance region vector with coefficient 0.4. In the spatial-twenty stress, use the corresponding twenty-degree blocks. These effects are redrawn between repetitions and shared within a repetition, including across splits. They are mean zero in the known population. The twenty-degree case tests dependence extending beyond the candidate five-degree weighting blocks; success at five degrees does not imply success at twenty.

The fixed text prototype is the unit-normalised vector A_c + 0.6 E[L_g | c is present in a test template] + 0.8 S_c. Beta is exactly zero or 0.4. Zero beta makes paired population image means identical and hence both paired population RSA differences exactly zero. Shared appearance and geography can still yield positive ordinary or partial RSA, so a marginal correlation under beta zero is not automatically a Type-I error. An independent-text diagnostic uses standard-normal text vectors independent of A, S, V and L, with the same fixed-world seed rule; its realised finite-class population correlation need not equal zero.

Analytic population class means average the conditional means equally over test templates containing the class. Analytic geographic histograms sum the actual class-window counts over all split templates. These are the exact expectations under the fresh stratified template population, because each split is sampled at its original cell count (or the common factor four). Scale does not change geographic cosine distances. Ordinary and partial Spearman RSA and the paired differences use the same frozen production rank/SVD formula as the preceding supplement. The population geographic matrix retains empirical zero bins and exact distance ties. Record tie counts and nuisance design rank; do not regularise or smooth ranks. Known truth comes from the specified latent population, independently of noisy replicate estimates and bootstrap draws.

## Resampling procedure and estimands

Within a repetition, average windows within each class-cell, then average represented cells equally for class prototypes. The geographic nuisance counts positive windows, as production does, using all splits. For each of 499 bootstrap draws generate independent Exp(1) positive weights for the sampled cells and normalise weights to mean one separately within each split. This split normalisation preserves the sampling design's fixed split sizes and prevents arbitrary random shifts in the relative archive contribution. Use each test cell's identical weight for both image routes, appearance and its geographic counts. Non-test cells contribute to the geographic histogram with their own weights. Rebuild all category means, histogram ranks and the nuisance SVD in every draw. Shared windows and shared endpoints of class-pair distances remain together. Never resample distances as independent observations.

For the spatial candidate, generate one Exp(1) weight per occupied five-degree block and assign it to every cell in that block across all splits; then apply the same split-wise normalisation. This preserves cross-split regional coupling. Five-degree blocks are fixed by coordinates before outcomes and never tuned to a variogram or observed effect. Report each class's represented cell count, represented block count and multiplier effective support (sum weights)^2/sum(weights^2), including its lower quantiles. A positive weight prevents support loss in a bootstrap draw, but cannot restore a class absent from the initial sample or establish information about unseen geography.

For each of ordinary baseline/semantic RSA, partial baseline/semantic RSA, and their two paired differences, let theta_hat be the point estimate and q be quantiles of rebuilt bootstrap estimates. The primary basic interval is [2 theta_hat - q_0.975, 2 theta_hat - q_0.025]. The comparator is [q_0.025, q_0.975]. Intersect correlation intervals with [-1,1] and difference intervals with [-2,2]; record any empty intersection as unavailable. Also preserve raw unbounded endpoints. Report theta_hat's bias and the diagnostic corrected-point bias for 2 theta_hat - mean(theta_star), with Monte Carlo standard errors. This correction is approximate and can itself fail for rare classes and discontinuous ranks.

One-sided paired tests are obtained from the corresponding 95% one-sided lower endpoint: 2 theta_hat - q_0.95 for basic and q_0.05 for percentile. Reject zero when this lower endpoint exceeds zero. This is explicitly different from the preceding supplement's null-centred p-value statistic; neither test is retroactively substituted into old results. Two-sided interval coverage and one-sided null size are assessed separately.

## Fixed scenarios, development and confirmation

Seven regimes (low_noise, standard, heterogeneous, spatial_five, spatial_twenty, fixed_design, support_x4) are crossed with beta in {0,0.4}: 14 scenarios. Add the standard fresh-sample independent-text beta-zero diagnostic, giving 15 scenarios. Cell-basic and block-basic are separately designated primary candidates for their respective declared dependence assumptions; both are always reported. Percentile results are comparators only. No candidate switch is made after development.

Development uses 64 repetitions per scenario and confirmation uses 1,000 repetitions per scenario. Repetition IDs and random seed namespaces are disjoint. Development is for implementation diagnostics, support/selection failure rates and a measured full-draw runtime. Any scientific change after development requires a new dated amendment and new independent confirmation seed namespace; old results remain archived. Confirmation uses the frozen complete implementation, configuration and input hashes. The same six population metrics and all four weight/interval combinations are reported, including failures. No model is tuned on the confirmation outcomes. The pilot uses two development repetitions per scenario with all 499 bootstrap draws.

## Acceptance and Monte Carlo uncertainty

Reuse the declared coverage band [0.92,0.995] and null size band [0.035,0.065]. Each proportion includes a Wilson 95% Monte Carlo interval and MCSE. Report the inherited point-rate criterion and, separately, a conservative confirmation criterion requiring the full Wilson interval inside the band. A result whose Monte Carlo uncertainty crosses a boundary remains inconclusive for conservative admission. No additional simulations are added selectively to move a result over a boundary. Missing/undefined intervals count as failures in the unconditional denominator. Admission-conditional and available-only coverage are diagnostics and cannot rescue unconditional failure.

Qualify ordinary geometry, partial geometry and paired differences separately for each weighting candidate and stated population/dependence regime. The exact qualifying scenario IDs are frozen in CONFIG.json. Cell-basic requires low_noise, standard and heterogeneous at both beta values plus independent_text_b0. Block-basic requires those seven scenarios plus spatial_five at both beta values. Null-size gates apply to the beta-zero IDs within each list. Each metric is qualified separately, with all its named scenarios required. There is no default empirical candidate and no outcome-based switch; candidate-specific reports state their distinct independence or five-degree dependence assumptions. The spatial_twenty scenarios are mandatory disclosed stresses and block an unqualified spatial or population claim. Passing the independent-cell cases does not establish empirical independence. Passing a paired null does not qualify a single-route population correlation. Power is reported against the known achieved positive differences with the inherited 0.8 reference; high power cannot rescue bad coverage or size. Fixed-design and support_x4 results cannot qualify the current-cohort new-location interval. If the current-support scenarios fail, leave the empirical population interval unadmitted and retain descriptive geometry.

## Outcome-blind acquisition fallback

If current support fails and support_x4 improves the relevant checks, plan an independent acquisition tranche by class/cell counts and geography alone. Prioritise the 25 classes with at most ten retained test cells, including the three with five, while preserving all 188 target classes and frozen train/test geographic separation. For illustration, a class with five cells needs 15 additional independent occupied cells to reach four times that support; a class with ten needs 30. These are simulation budget examples, not an acceptance threshold or permission to change the original admission gate. Report the full per-class deficit to a separately declared sampling target, the number of new cells and windows, cap implications and expected acquisition cost before deployment. Use new held-out cells independent of interval-development and representation-effect decisions. If increased support still fails, require a different declared estimator or retain descriptive results.

## Statistical sources and limits

Rubin (1981), [The Bayesian Bootstrap](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~jordan/sail/readings/rubin.pdf), defines random positive probability weights; its posterior interpretation does not establish frequentist coverage here. Præstgaard and Wellner (1993), [Exchangeably Weighted Bootstraps of the General Empirical Process](https://faculty.washington.edu/jonw/JAW-papers/jaw-praest-93AP.pdf), gives conditions for weighted empirical-process convergence; rare finite support and ranked nuisance degeneracy require separate checks.

The basic interval follows bootstrap-error inversion as presented in Wasserman's [All of Statistics, Section 8.3](https://www.stat.cmu.edu/~brian/valerie/617-2022/0%20-%20books/2004%20-%20wasserman%20-%20all%20of%20statistics.pdf). It is an approximate pivot, not a general cure for bias. Hesterberg (2015), [What Teachers Should Know About the Bootstrap](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4784504/), discusses small-sample and reverse-percentile limitations. Positive Dirichlet weights can understate finite-sample variability; no empirical variance factor is inserted to force calibration.

Schütt et al. (2023), [Statistical inference on representational geometries](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.82566), distinguishes generalisation over sampled factors and measurement-noise contributions. Its two-factor neuroscience procedure is not assumed to validate this unequal-support geographic design. Kurisu, Kato and Shao (2024), [Gaussian approximation and spatially dependent wild bootstrap for high-dimensional spatial data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10720), establishes results under stated spatial sampling and dependence assumptions. It motivates explicit spatial stresses; it does not supply a theorem for positive block weights and partial rank RSA. This amendment therefore depends on independent simulation coverage within its declared population, with all extrapolation limits retained.
